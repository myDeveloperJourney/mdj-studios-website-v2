A first look · no experience needed

A supplier asks to move a delivery. You could paste that email into a chatbot and ask what it says and what to write back. Or you could leave a helper in charge of the inbox and only hear from it when a real decision is waiting.

Those two paths look similar until you watch who starts the work, what remembers the job, and where you still have to show up. That difference is the cleanest way I know to separate chat AI from agentic AI.

I built a side-by-side walkthrough for this. The metaphor below is the same one that demo uses.

## The coworker at the desk, and the assistant with a job

A chatbot is a smart coworker at their desk. When you walk over, they help. When you leave, they wait. They are sharp about whatever you put in front of them. They do not keep watching the room for you.

An agent is an assistant you hired for one job. Watch the mail. Sort it. Knock only when they need a yes. They do not need you to restart the conversation every time a new message arrives. The job is saved. Something can wake it while you are away.

If a person still has to start it every time, it is a chat. If something wakes a saved job that can actually do things, people call that an agent.

## Side by side

| | Asking a chatbot | A helper with a job |
|---|---|---|
| Who starts it | You, every time | New mail or a clock; you do not have to be there |
| Memory | No. Close the window, and the job is done | Yes. A saved job can run while you are away |
| What it can touch | Only what you paste. It talks. You do the rest | Open the inbox, file mail, write a draft |
| Where you fit | Almost every step | The decision: should this be sent? |
| Next email | Start over | Already watching |

Chat is a conversation you keep opening. An agentic workflow is a job you set up once, then steer with judgment.

## Three emails from a fictional class inbox

Imagine a training inbox with a few ordinary messages. Nothing fancy. Just enough to see the pattern.

### 1. Delivery change

Priya from Northwind asks to move a delivery.

**Chat path:** You notice the mail. You paste it into the chatbot. You ask for a draft. You send it. You file the thread. Every step waits on you.

**Agent path:** The helper is already watching. It drafts a reply. It asks for a yes before anything goes out. After you approve, it files the thread and keeps watching.

Same email. Different who-owns-the-loop.

### 2. Junk newsletter

A newsletter you do not want.

**Chat path:** You still have to open it, decide it is junk, and trash it. The chatbot only helps if you bring it over.

**Agent path:** The helper can trash it without pinging you. Not every message deserves a knock on the door.

### 3. FYI pallet note

A short note that something landed. No decision needed.

**Chat path:** If you ask, it can summarize. Then it waits until you walk over again.

**Agent path:** It files the note and stays quiet. Your attention stays free for the messages that need a human call.

## Why this matters for tech professionals

I have been teaching since 2017 and have trained 1000+ professionals. The question I hear most is not "which model is smartest." It is "when do I still do the work, and when can a system carry the loop?"

Chat AI is great when you want a sharp answer on demand. Agentic workflows matter when the work has a trigger, a memory, tools that can act, and a clear place for your judgment. That is the shift: from talking to a smart coworker to hiring a helper for a job you can actually run.

If you are leveling up in the agentic AI era, start with one boring inbox job. Decide what should wake the helper. Decide what needs a yes from you. Decide what can stay quiet. Then build the workflow around those calls.

That is agentic work in practice. Not a slogan. A loop you can leave running.
